# Product

## Register

dual

**Brand surfaces** (homepage, about, case studies, network, mission, learning sites, blog):
Design IS the product. The public site is the primary credibility instrument — it must demonstrate rigour, authenticity, and earned authority before a single word is read.

**Product surfaces** (admin, donations, events, community, auth):
Design SERVES the product. Clarity, speed, and reduced cognitive load. Never ornamental.

## Users

Three overlapping audiences arrive on the same public surface:

**Ecologists and conservationists** — professionals, researchers, and volunteers who might partner with, work on, or contribute expertise to restoration sites. They come literate. They will notice if the science is thin or the language is vague.

**Donors and funders** — philanthropists, foundations, and impact investors. They need to believe the work is real, measurable, and well-governed. Credibility is the conversion lever, not emotion.

**General public** — curious readers discovering ecological restoration for the first time. Awareness is the win. They should leave with a changed sense of what's possible.

All three audiences need to feel that the work is legitimate, grounded, and free of the performative activism that defines most organisations in this space.

## Product Purpose

Barbets Duet is a global ecological restoration collective. The site exists to legitimise and amplify the network's work — documenting real restoration sites, surfacing measurable outcomes, and making the case that ecological recovery at scale is both urgent and achievable.

Success looks like: a funder trusting the numbers, a conservationist wanting to contribute their site, a general reader feeling the weight of what's being lost and the credibility of what's being built.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous. Grounded. Expressive.

The brand voice is a natural history journal written by someone who is also moved to action. Precise language, not bureaucratic. Editorial confidence, not activist urgency. The visual language earns its expressiveness through restraint — sparse accent colour, heavy typography, measured whitespace.

Three words: **scholarly**, **alive**, **uncompromising**.

Emotional goal: the feeling you get when you read something important for the first time and know it.

## Anti-references

**Generic charity / NGO sites** — stock photo hero, thin typography, pastel greens, "Together we can" copy. Screams performative. No credibility with expert audiences.

**Tech startup / SaaS minimalism** — Inter, blue-purple gradients, feature grids with emoji, "Get started free" CTAs. This is the opposite register. Nothing in common with ecological science.

**Greenwashing corporate sustainability** — Fortune 500 ESG pages. Glossy stock of trees + corporate colour palettes + zero specificity. The anti-pattern this site exists to contrast.

**Nature photography portfolio** — full-bleed imagery with minimal text. More gallery than editorial. Aesthetics without argument.

**Sector clichés of any kind** — avoid all signals that pattern-match to "environmental charity": green-on-white, leaf icons, "making a difference", exclamation marks, round numbers, aspirational stock photography of untouched wilderness. Authenticity means specificity: real sites, real names, real data.

## Design Principles

1. **Specificity over aspiration.** Real site names, real coordinates, real outcome data. Vague claims corrode credibility faster than silence. If a number can't be sourced, remove it.

2. **Earn the accent.** The Neon Lime accent should feel like a discovery, not a brand wallpaper. One CTA, one active state. If it appears more than twice on a screen, it's wrong.

3. **Typography carries the argument.** BioRhyme headings should feel like the title of a paper worth reading. The writing and the typesetting are inseparable. Never let a layout make the text feel decorative.

4. **Dual-register discipline.** Marketing surfaces get editorial tension and compositional risk. App surfaces get clarity and restraint. Never cross the wires — no bento grids in the donations flow, no generic form layouts on the homepage.

5. **Authenticity as the design system.** The real anti-pattern isn't ugly design — it's design that signals "we are trying to look legitimate". The goal is design that makes legitimacy invisible because everything confirms it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum across all surfaces. Dark mode is the primary surface; light mode receives equal contrast validation.

Body text: ≥ 4.5:1 against background in both themes.
Large text (≥ 18px): ≥ 3:1.
Focus indicators: always structural (underline or outline), never colour-only.
Motion: `prefers-reduced-motion` respected on all animations; content never gated behind a transition.
Colour blindness: accent colour (Neon Lime) is never the sole information carrier.
