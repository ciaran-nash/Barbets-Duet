**Who We Are** We are the Barbet’s Duet, an experiment in systemic invention and a global *Jumuiya* (a Kiswahili term for a collective or constellation) of learning sites. Operating across disparate environments in East Africa, the United Kingdom, and the United States, our collective consists of like-minded individuals and families working to restore ecological function on their land while creating viable economic livelihoods.  
**The Unsynchronized Duet** We are named after the Crested Barbet (*Trachyphonus vaillantii*), a tropical bird whose song is famously not synchronized. This biological phenomenon symbolises our operational structure: distinct voices from African and Western, modern and traditional cultures singing together on equal terms without losing their individual identities. We believe that dual cultural engagement is essential to maximising our learning and solving complex environmental challenges.  
**Solving the "Oak Tree Paradox"** We exist to solve a fundamental economic flaw: a mature oak tree supports hundreds of species, providing food, habitat, and immense 'life' value, but its financial value is only realised once it is cut down. The Barbet’s Duet operates on the belief that environmental care must shift from being a philanthropic gift to an economic necessity. Explicitly defining ourselves as "a business idea, not a charity," our mission is to invent new institutional rules and market mechanisms that reward the abundance of life rather than its destruction.  
**A Methodology of Trial and Error** We do not present a polished, corporate narrative of success. Healing the land is a messy, complex process, and we prioritise radical honesty through our methodology of "Trial and Error". We require our members to openly document their work by answering specific prompts: *"What did you try and how did it turn out?"*, *"What was your biggest mistake?"*, and *"What did you learn and what made you laugh?"*. Making and sharing these mistakes is an essential, inevitable precursor to genuine systemic invention.  
**Footpaths Over Fences: Mosaic Rights** Our work is guided by the traditional African land tenure framework known as "Mosaic Rights"—a system characterised by footpaths and shared, distributed access that historically yields high social equity and high biodiversity. We contrast this with the Western system of "Column Rights"—characterised by fences and exclusive, vertical ownership. By exploring how to integrate the inclusive principles of Mosaic Rights into modern property regimes, we aim to ensure that human communities and the natural world can function symbiotically.  
**A Multi-Generational Experiment** Formalised at our first "Invention Convention" in Mlingotini, Tanzania, in 2009, this project was initially conceived as a 20-year experiment. However, our founding partners quickly realised that true environmental restoration and systemic invention cannot be bound by deadlines; it is a multi-generational effort. Today, we are actively integrating a younger generation into our *Jumuiya*, ensuring that the practical work of healing the land and our shared dream of an equitable, regenerative future will continue long after us
